# Canadian field-data research notes — 2026-09-27 UTC

This is discovery and sample inspection, not runtime admission, benchmark activation, agronomic validation, or a count of independent fields. The 23 catalog records include eight UBC Farm datasets from **one farm**, five SMAPVEX16 products from **one campaign**, eight federal products with different observation/model/aggregate roles, one gated long-term experiment database, and one Dryad synthesis dataset. Counting catalog records as independent farms would be wrong.

## Best field-record candidate

[UBC Farm's Borealis collection](https://borealisdata.ca/dataverse/UBC_CSFS) links management records by field/bed and a [dated field map](https://doi.org/10.5683/SP2/ZIOMGM). I queried the *individual dataset metadata APIs* for cover crops (`Y1G0PX`), seed/transplants (`QSXH6H`), IPM (`SMNMGF`), soil amendment (`QOP670`), land-use map (`ZIOMGM`), click beetles (`WL0DXB`), spotted wing drosophila (`BMIFBN`), and peppermint intercropping (`HMH80N`). Each `latestVersion.license` was **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** on 2026-09-27. This is source-specific evidence; it does not grant commercial use. Two research-only CSV files were retrieved from the `api/access/datafile/{id}?format=original` route into ignored `data/raw/field_data_research/20260927/canada/`. Their full checksums, byte counts, rows, and columns are in `canada-sources.json`.

The two `.tab` files are actually **comma-delimited**, not tab-delimited. Cover crops: 449 rows, 21 columns, 28 distinct `field_code` strings in 2019–2026; the first row records 2019-03-11, `D7-3`, ryegrass and 2,818.56 ft². Seed/transplants: 2,233 rows, 27 columns, 36 distinct `field` strings; the first row is celery, Kelvin (organic pelleted), sown 2021-05-07, field `D7`. The latter has `plan_date`, `completed`, `seed_date`, and `date_field_transplant`, so a planned action is not automatically an observed completed action. The cover-crop notes themselves include an uncertain field-map placement. A demo should preserve that conflict instead of asserting an exact polygon join. The sampled cover-crop file has 2019–2026; the dataset also lists a 2010–2018 file that was **not downloaded**.

Candidate questions grounded in the inspected UBC bytes (drafts, not held-out benchmark answers):

- Easy: “What cover crop was recorded in `D7-3` on 2019-03-11?” The row says ryegrass and 2,818.56 ft². Its `first_planting` and `second_planting` are blank; do not fill them from another row.
- Medium: “Which beds in `D7-3` had a rye, crimson-clover, vetch mixture on 2020-10-07?” Three rows cover beds 1–5, 6–10, and 11–15, each with 7,046.4 ft². They are separate bed ranges, not duplicate rows.
- Hard: “How did the `D7-3` cover-crop records change between 2020-06-20 and 2020-10-07?” The June row covers beds 14–17 and lists rye, crimson clover, and white clover plus additional species in free-text notes; the October rows cover beds 1–15 and list rye, crimson clover, and vetch. Beds 14–15 overlap the dated ranges. These are recorded plantings, not evidence that the first mixture failed or was terminated.
- Expert: “Can the 2021 seed/transplant file establish that all peppers planned for `D7-3` were planted and what yield followed?” It contains 25 `completed=True` records in the `field` string `D7-3` across crops, including peppers with March sowing and May transplant dates, but it also contains planned/incomplete rows, no plot yield, and no exact GIS join in this sample. The answer must enumerate matching rows and state the outcome gap rather than infer yield or completion from dates alone.

## Other useful Canadian evidence

The [AAFC Annual Crop Inventory Ground Truth Data](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/503a3113-e435-49f4-850c-d70056788632) is especially useful for crop-label and geometry ingestion. [Annual Crop Inventory](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/ba2645d5-4458-414d-b196-6303ac06c1c9), [2023 detected field boundaries](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/b3dba669-6611-4cb1-b4be-6c317b9908ad), [rotation characteristics](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/41f33e82-c19c-481e-8873-534b5e6d7ebc), and [100 m soil grids](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/4d39c9f9-a85c-4bf2-b920-138fdd423384) are **model/map products**, not independent observed management, soil tests, or yield. [Small-area field-crop reporting](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/ae9c1230-e2dc-4155-a68b-6d521f57baaa) is aggregate context, not a field outcome. Their individual government records show the Open Government Licence - Canada; source-specific download directories are in the JSON catalog. The government directories were not bulk-downloaded or inspected here.

[DRIVES](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/diverse-rotations-improve-valuable-ecosystem-services-drives-database) is unusually rich for yield, management, weather and experimental units, including Elora and Ridgetown in Ontario. I downloaded **only** its public `site_info.csv`: 22 rows, 29 columns, first two rows are the Canadian sites. The publisher explicitly requires an ordinary data-request form and additional Canadian site forms for substantive tables, plus acknowledgement in products/publications. Its catalog's CC0 license for the public repository must not be interpreted as permission to fetch or redistribute gated Canadian yield/management tables. Those tables remain unavailable in this review.

The [Bowles et al. Dryad rotation dataset](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi%3A10.6078/D1H409) lists a 567 KB CSV of plot/block maize yields from 11 U.S./Canadian long-term trials. The owner independently verified **CC0-1.0** in this exact DOI's [DataCite rights metadata](https://api.datacite.org/dois/10.6078/D1H409), with a saved raw-metadata SHA256 receipt recorded in the JSON. The data file itself was not downloaded in this pass. Identify Canadian site codes and overlap with DRIVES before treating the rows as independent benchmark fields.

[AAFC's Swift Current long-term tillage study](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/b22cd297-cdb4-4d76-9f79-cc1c16d0e9e7) is a better commercial-compatible measured soil/hydrology lead. Its official dataset metadata explicitly gives the Open Government Licence - Canada, with downloadable management descriptions, soil nutrients, soil moisture, runoff, nitrate, phosphorus and other CSVs for an edge-of-field watershed study from 1962–2011. I downloaded two tiny files: `Management_System_Details.csv` has 5 rows and 4 columns of **typical** practice descriptions; `Soil_Nutrients_1970-1992_1_1_1.csv` has 93 rows and 11 columns of watershed/date/depth and nutrient values. Their `é` text requires Latin-1 decoding despite portal metadata saying UTF-8. One soil row reports watershed 1, `Year_Année=70`, month 10, day 2, depth code 1, nitrate 3.75, ammoniacal N 6.4, phosphorus 9.68. The two-digit year, depth code and units require its data dictionary; I have not interpreted them. There is **no yield file** among this dataset's listed resources, and its typical management description is not a dated field operation.

The five [NSIDC SMAPVEX16 Manitoba products](https://nsidc.org/data/sv16m_csm/versions/1) in the catalog provide actual soil cores, probes, station/RISMA measurements, vegetation samples, and soil texture over a 2016 campaign. Each page requires a free NASA Earthdata Login and dataset citation. I found no source-specific reuse license in this pass and downloaded none. They are a valuable expert case for point versus field inference and cross-product spatial/temporal joins, but they do not supply management chronology or grain yield.

## Bounded attempts and unresolved work

- Targeted searches for downloadable plot-yield tables at Swift Current, Melfort, Lethbridge, Glenlea, Elora and Ridgetown found no additional open primary table beyond the cataloged Bowles and gated DRIVES sources. This is bounded search non-discovery, not proof of absence. [University of Manitoba's Glenlea study](https://www.umanitoba.ca/agricultural-food-sciences/long-term-agronomic-studies/glenlea-long-term-rotation) says archived grain/soil samples and data are available by contacting its coordinator. [GLTEN's Lethbridge ABC record](https://www.glten.org/experiments/205) lists data access as “Don't know.” Neither is a verified downloadable plot-data source for this catalog.
- Direct `web.open` requests to Borealis and Open Government pages returned HTTP 403 in the browsing tool. Search-index snippets exposed the source pages, while an authorized small metadata/API retrieval confirmed UBC dataset licenses and file IDs. Initial ordinary shell networking failed with DNS restriction; an escalated, bounded network call succeeded. No bulk transfer was attempted.
- The eight UBC Farm records are source-distinct *datasets* but share a farm and may share rows/field codes. The five SMAPVEX16 records share a campaign. Deduplicate by site, field, year, and experimental lineage before training/evaluation splits.
- No government GeoJSON, Shapefile, raster, or NASA product was downloaded; only the two tiny Swift Current federal CSVs. No field polygons were joined to the UBC samples. No held-out benchmark answers were authored from these files. Their exact access, join cardinality, and row quality remain unknown.
- A noncommercial ShareAlike license constrains UBC samples; NASA rights were not established; DRIVES protected tables require a specific request. These are gating conditions for a distributable demo or future training, even though limited local research inspection succeeded. Bowles' exact DOI metadata establishes CC0 for that dataset, but Canadian site rows and overlap still need inspection.
