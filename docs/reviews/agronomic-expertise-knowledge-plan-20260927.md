# Agronomic expertise layer for field data and small local models

Status: source and system design candidate, coordinated with the separate field-data ingestion task. It is not a runtime admission, training authorization, or claim that the model is an agronomist.

## What the current evidence says

The active offline store is strongest in Canadian provincial/soil context and a large, separately gated U.S. ecological-site archive. The inspected Prairie SQLite layers contain mapped soil polygons and components, not a field's actual nutrient supply, crop management, yield, or finances. The source-linked nine-card pilot in this branch made technical methods retrievable, but a leakage-free 13-question paired model run did not reliably repair arithmetic or verifier fallbacks. That makes a larger pile of short RAG cards a poor substitute for typed equations, input checks, and field-specific evidence.

The useful target is **bounded expert behavior**: explain the mechanism; identify the exact observation and method needed; calculate from typed user inputs; compare plausible interpretations; expose uncertainty and local authority; and withhold field decisions when calibration, labels, or current data are missing. A small model can orchestrate that work if the hard parts live in a source-governed evidence layer and deterministic tools.

## Knowledge representation

Store each technical claim as a source-bound record with `publisher | exact URL and byte hash | section/page/table locator | publication/review date | rights | geography/crop/system | concept/equation | symbol/unit definitions | assumptions | method dependencies | calibration boundary | evidence role | falsifier or update trigger`. Source prose, extracted equations, project interpretation, and a field observation remain separate records. Do not turn a PDF title, map attribute, trial mean, or model prediction into an unconditional recommendation.

An equation needs a typed contract: units, denominators, value ranges, missingness, uncertainty propagation where possible, and a worked **source-native or newly generated non-evaluation** example outside runtime if necessary. Evaluation questions and answers stay in a separate partition. Candidate documents require rights review before source-text redistribution; a project-authored paraphrase is clearly labelled as such. A knowledge graph can link concepts, methods, crops, and observations, but graph relationships do not upgrade source authority.

## First technical modules

| Module and source candidates | Deep concepts to preserve | Deterministic or structured capability | Hard question to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Soil chemistry and laboratory methods.** [USDA NRCS Kellogg laboratory guidance](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/resources/guides-and-instructions/kellogg-soil-survey-laboratory-guidance), including SSIR 42/45, alongside Canadian provincial soil-test calibration documents. | pH, buffering, CEC, exchangeable bases, extractant/method dependence, depth, units, P availability versus total P. A result is operationally defined by its method; two labs' values may not be directly comparable. | Method-aware soil-test normalizer and compatibility check; unit conversion only when analyte, basis, depth and method permit it. | “Did soil P change?” with different extraction methods and depths; correct answer may be unknown. |
| **Nutrient cycles, budgets, and response.** [NRCS National Agronomy Manual Part 503](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2022-10/National-Agronomy-Manual.pdf), [NRCS nutrient management](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/getting-assistance/other-topics/nutrient-management), provincial calibration and crop-removal tables. | Mineralization, immobilization, uptake/removal, source availability, legume/manure credits, loss pathways, soil-test calibration, response curves, economic optimum, timing and placement. | Nutrient mass-balance ledger with source, timing, basis and uncertainty; response-curve comparison only with locally fit trials and current prices. | Repeated tissue rows must not triple an application; observational N rates cannot establish a causal yield response. |
| **Water, irrigation, and soil physics.** [NRCS Irrigation Guide Part 652](https://directives.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files2/1720464454/Part%20652%20-%20Irrigation%20Guide.pdf), AAFC agroclimate methods, field sensor metadata. | Root-zone storage, infiltration/runoff, evapotranspiration, drainage, capillary contribution, texture/structure, sensor depth and calibration. | Daily water-balance calculator with explicit precipitation, irrigation, ET, runoff and deep-percolation terms; missing terms stay unknown. | Is a remote moisture anomaly water stress or sensor/soil-depth mismatch? |
| **Crop establishment and phenology.** [NRCS National Agronomy Manual](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2022-10/National-Agronomy-Manual.pdf), [Manitoba seeding-rate guidance](https://www.gov.mb.ca/agriculture/crops/seasonal-reports/pubs/seeding-rates-spring-cereals.pdf), [AAFC growing-degree-day service](https://agriculture.canada.ca/imagery-images/rest/services/agclimate/growing_degree_days/ImageServer). | Viable seed, emergence survival, thousand-kernel weight, stand density, thermal-time base and caps, crop stage versus calendar date, cultivar/environment interaction. | Existing `seed_rate_mass` and `daily_gdd` calculators need broader natural-language input parsing, unit conversion and final-answer receipt parity. | Calculate a seed rate from user values without inventing a target or misusing a different seed lot. |
| **On-farm trials, causal inference, and statistics.** [University of Minnesota on-farm research guide](https://extension.umn.edu/courses-and-events/for-professionals/on-farm-research/how-to-do-research-on-your-farm) and source-specific study protocols. | Treatment versus observational association, randomization, replication, blocking, experimental unit, spatial autocorrelation, missing plots, uncertainty and transfer to a different field. | Trial-design validator; grouped estimators with plot/site/year lineage; no pseudoreplication. | Does an apparent yield difference survive blocks and independent sites? |
| **Imagery and geospatial measurement.** [HLS v2 user guide](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/1698/HLS_User_Guide_V2.pdf), [USGS quality-mask documentation](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-4-7-surface-reflectance-quality-assessment), AAFC Annual Crop Inventory specifications. | Surface reflectance versus index, cloud/shadow/snow masks, scale factors, geolocation, edge mixing, clear-pixel denominator, phenology-matched history and crop-class uncertainty. | Windowed COG reader, QA-mask interpreter, valid-area aggregation, as-of time filtering; never infer yield from NDVI alone. | Does a satellite anomaly persist after cloud, boundary, crop and date controls? |
| **Crop and whole-farm economics.** [USDA ERS commodity-cost methodology](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/commodity-costs-and-returns/documentation), [farm financial ratios](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/farm-income-and-wealth-statistics/documentation-for-the-farm-sector-financial-ratios), [Manitoba budget formulas](https://www.gov.mb.ca/agriculture/farm-management/cost-production/pubs/cop-special-crop-production-v2.pdf), [Iowa State partial budgeting](https://www.extension.iastate.edu/Agdm/wholefarm/html/c1-50.html). | Cash versus accrual, operating versus total economic cost, inventory, opportunity cost, machinery ownership, contribution margin, partial budget, working capital, liquidity and solvency. Current price/contract/rate inputs are separate live or user data. | Typed budget, break-even and ratio calculators with cost-boundary labels and sensitivity tables; no silent switch of denominator. | A positive crop margin can coexist with a seasonal cash shortfall; a total cost must not be relabelled operating cost. |
| **Crop protection and regulated actions.** [AAFC western field-pest guide](https://agriculture.canada.ca/en/agricultural-production/crop-protection/agricultural-pest-management-resources/field-crop-and-forage-pests-and-their-natural-enemies-western-canada-identification-and-management), regional extension scouting methods, current Canadian PMRA/U.S. EPA labels. | Pest identification, host/crop stage, scouting denominator, economic injury/threshold uncertainty, natural enemies, resistance and label jurisdiction. | Scouting evidence checklist and live-label authority adapter; no offline product permission or rate from a historical guide. | Is treatment justified from one photograph or an unverified count? Usually not. |

These are source *candidates*. Check each edition, licence, page/section, author and scope before deriving distributable text. The repository already contains some related Canadian guidance, so deduplicate and add only missing methods or deeper, source-distinct explanations. Formal standards, current labels, crop-insurance rules and market data need a currency and authority path rather than timeless static text.

## Coupling to the field-data work

The field-data task is building upload/review, field records, and a benchmark from real datasets. We have asked that task for its normalized schema, admitted dataset families and file ownership. The interface between efforts should be a typed **evidence question**: field/season/plot identifier, observation time, unit and basis, method and instrument, quality/ambiguity status, source-file locator, and whether a record is measured, planned, inferred, or modelled. The knowledge layer supplies general method and applicability; the field layer supplies a user's observations. They must be joined only through a validated question-specific binding, with each side's source identity preserved.

Use the peer task's real data to test the first boundaries: repeated management values across sample rows, incompatible soil methods, missing yield, anonymized geometry, treatment versus field identity, and as-of imagery. These become **case-local uploads**, never general RAG facts. Rights for noncommercial or access-controlled datasets remain source-specific; their availability in a research catalog is not runtime or training admission.

## Benchmark before expansion

Freeze a small source-distinct development set, then separately reserve independently authored/reviewed evaluation fields and questions. Keep every year and derivative of one physical field/site in the same split for the unseen-field test. For each domain compare the same question under: local model alone; model plus admitted reference methods; reference methods plus deterministic calculators; reviewed field records plus those capabilities; and raw-upload/full product path. This separates a language-memory failure from retrieval, extraction, numeric tooling, field identity, and verification failures.

Score source applicability, correct method/equation, units and denominator, numeric result, uncertainty, field/source provenance, abstention when inputs are missing, cross-border/legal boundary, and operational latency. Human-reviewed numeric oracles and source locators are essential; a model judge is triage only. Retain wrong answers, nulls, timeouts, fallbacks, and source cards that were retrieved but ignored. Do not write the test's synthetic input numbers or expected answers into runtime cards: the pilot review found and removed exactly that leakage.

The next implementation unit should be **one method family plus one typed calculator plus one source-distinct field-data benchmark lane**, likely nutrient accounting or crop establishment. Run the corpus-only arm first. If the calculator/record arm improves the frozen cases without authority regression, then test once on a new source/site cohort. Do not fine-tune until the source and field-data partitions, rights, and answer rubric are proven; otherwise the model can memorize exposed examples without learning agronomy.

## Decision

The nine-card pilot stays non-active. It proves that technical source records can be made reachable, and it exposes the harder bottleneck: extraction of typed inputs, deterministic math, verifier interpretation, and field-specific applicability. Treat the modules above as a staged source/tool/evaluation backlog, not a claim that context alone turns a small language model into a professional agronomist.
