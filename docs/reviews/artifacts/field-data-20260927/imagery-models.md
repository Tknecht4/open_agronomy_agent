# Field imagery and local EO model feasibility

Checked 2026-09-27 UTC. Research-only proposal; no model installed, weights downloaded, imagery acquired, or inference benchmark run. Observation means inspected source or owner-reported live machine fact; estimates and proposals are labelled. Source/query ledger: [imagery-sources.json](imagery-sources.json).

## Recommendation

Build the first current-season dashboard from QA-filtered HLS/Sentinel-2 field observations, dates, clear-pixel coverage, and simple vegetation-index trajectories. Trial small frozen encoders only as an additional feature source. For HLS specifically, Prithvi EO 2.0 tiny-TL is the most direct input match; for native Sentinel-2 time series, OlmoEarth v1.2 Nano/Tiny and Presto are the bounded local candidates. An embedding is a model output, not a yield measurement, diagnosis, or ready-made yield forecast.

The owner inspected the host: Apple M4, `hw.memsize=17179869184` (16 GiB); project environment has MLX 0.32.2 but no installed `torch`, `rasterio`, or `xarray`. Thus CPU/MPS feasibility below remains unmeasured. MLX availability does not make these PyTorch models MLX-compatible.

## HLS access, cost, and field windows

**Yes, HLS is on the AWS Registry of Open Data.** Both products use controlled-access `lp-prod-protected` prefixes, `HLSL30.020` and `HLSS30.020`, in `us-west-2`; this is not an anonymous public bucket. The registry marks the HLS data CC BY 4.0. [L30 registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/nasa-hlsl30/), [S30 registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/nasa-hlss30/).

Earthdata Login authenticates downloads. Direct S3 uses temporary, same-region read-only credentials: a local Mac should use authenticated HTTPS asset URLs instead of assuming those S3 credentials allow out-of-region access. CMR/CMR-STAC discovers items by geometry/time and returns asset links. AppEEARS offers spatial/temporal/band subsets; NASA also lists a Planetary Computer mirror, whose current coverage and access terms still need a separate item-level check. No AWS account or paid EC2 instance is required merely to download HLS by the NASA HTTPS route. The inspected HLS listing is controlled access, not labelled requester pays. [NASA access overview](https://hls.gsfc.nasa.gov/data-access-and-tools/), [LP DAAC credential documentation](https://data.lpdaac.earthdatacloud.nasa.gov/s3credentialsREADME), [NASA direct-S3 support clarification](https://forum.earthdata.nasa.gov/viewtopic.php?t=3995).

HLS L30/S30 are harmonized 30 m surface-reflectance products on common MGRS grids, supplied as a separate COG per band plus QA. Global land coverage includes agricultural Canada and the U.S.; L30 begins in 2013 and S30 in 2015. Nominal combined observations every 2–3 days are not clear observations every 2–3 days. The April 2026 guide describes typical 1–2 day processing, while the current access page says roughly 2–3 days; expose actual acquisition and availability dates instead of promising a latency. [HLS guide, sections 2, 5, 6](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/1698/HLS_User_Guide_V2.pdf), [NASA access page](https://hls.gsfc.nasa.gov/data-access-and-tools/).

Proposed acquisition flow: validate a field polygon and CRS; buffer in metres in a suitable local projection; discover intersecting items for the crop-season interval; retain item IDs, product/version, acquisition time, asset URLs and scale/offset; read only selected COG bands and pixel windows through range requests; reproject once onto a fixed grid; retain the unbuffered field mask separately. COG access fetches compressed internal blocks, so a small window is not a guarantee of equally small network traffic. Record actual transferred bytes and requests. A field crossing a tile/UTM boundary requires deliberate mosaicking. Keep cropped bytes and transform/hash receipts locally; avoid archiving entire tiles by default.

For the harmonized six-band stack use blue, green, red, narrow NIR, SWIR1, SWIR2; preserve sensor-specific mapping, not alphabetical asset order. Mask fill, cloud, adjacent cloud/shadow, shadow, snow/ice and high aerosol as applicable, and retain exclusion reasons. Apply reflectance scale 0.0001, offset 0; the registry documents missing scale metadata in some granules, historical Fmask problems, duplicates, geolocation problems and spring snow artifacts. Never use scene-wide cloud percentage as the field's valid-pixel fraction. [HLS L30 known issues](https://registry.opendata.aws/nasa-hlsl30/), [S30 known issues](https://registry.opendata.aws/nasa-hlss30/).

A 30 m pixel covers 0.09 ha: a 10 ha field contains at most about 111 pixel areas before boundary/cloud losses. Small or narrow fields can be dominated by mixed pixels. Keep fractional-area weights and an interior-only sensitivity summary. A buffer supplies spatial context to a model; it must not silently enlarge the area used for field statistics. Show clear observed fraction, last clear date, valid count, sensor and resolution alongside every map/index.

**Input mismatch is a larger initial risk than weights fitting.** OlmoEarth's minimal example uses 12 ordered Sentinel-2 L2A bands, normalization, `(B,H,W,T,C)` tensors, timestamps, masks and `input_res=10`; derived maps are decoder-only training targets. HLS is harmonized/resampled and its six-band stack is not a drop-in native Sentinel-2 tensor. Use the documented native modality or validate an explicitly versioned adaptation. Prithvi's HLS stack offers a cleaner first HLS experiment. [Minimal package](https://github.com/allenai/olmoearth_pretrain_minimal), [materialization tutorial](https://github.com/allenai/rslearn/blob/master/docs/examples/OlmoEarthEmbeddings.md).

OlmoEarth training documentation assumes distributed NVIDIA/CUDA infrastructure, but that does not prove inference requires CUDA. Its main dependency file makes FlashAttention optional, and the minimal package is much smaller. The inspected minimal `pyproject.toml` changed between indexed views (0.0.8 and 0.1.0), so documentation is not a lockfile. CPU examples support a first CPU trial; no inspected source establishes tested M4/MPS support. PyTorch supplies a general MPS device with `is_available()` checks; moving tensors is only the beginning of a compatibility test. ## Transparent memory estimates, not measurements

Example: a square 100 ha field is 1,000 m per side; add 200 m context on each side to obtain a 1,400 m square. Ignoring alignment padding this is `ceil(1400/30)=47` or `1400/10=140` pixels per side. Raw float32 tensor bytes are `B × H × W × T × C × 4`; below B=1 and MiB=2^20 bytes.

| Input | 1 date | 12 dates | 24 dates |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 47×47, 6 bands, 30 m example | 0.051 MiB | 0.607 MiB | 1.213 MiB |
| 140×140, 6 bands, 10 m comparison | 0.449 MiB | 5.383 MiB | 10.767 MiB |
| 224×224, 6 bands, model window | 1.148 MiB | 13.781 MiB | 27.562 MiB |
| 128×128, 12 bands, Sentinel-2 model input | 0.750 MiB | 9.000 MiB | 18.000 MiB |
| 256×256, 12 bands | 3.000 MiB | 36.000 MiB | 72.000 MiB |

Int16 storage before decompression is half those values; float16 is also half, but is not presumed numerically/runtime-safe. Masks, lat/lon/time encodings, copies, warp buffers, COG blocks, weights and activations are extra. A 140×140×64 float32 annual embedding crop is 4.785 MiB. Storing one mean vector per field is tiny, but loses within-field variation.

Encoder weight-only lower bounds at FP32 are approximately 6.5 MiB for 1.7M, 47.7 MiB for 12.5M, 135.8 MiB for 35.6M, 434.9 MiB for 114M, 1.118 GiB for 300M. FP16 halves these arithmetic bounds. Actual checkpoint and peak resident memory can be much larger. For an illustrative dense attention tensor, 3,072 tokens × 3,072 tokens × 8 heads × 4 bytes is 288 MiB for a single layer's attention matrix; optimized/factorized attention may differ. Consequently small raw image crops do not establish a memory upper bound. A 16 GiB unified pool also serves macOS, browser, database and the language model.

## A bounded local experiment to run later

No cells below have been measured. Do not install into the serving environment during research. Use a separate pinned environment and allowlist exact model/config hashes and preprocessing versions.

| Order | Case at batch 1 | Devices/dtypes | Acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Same field/time QA-index pipeline, no EO encoder | CPU float32 | Valid-pixel masks, hashes, dates and deterministic field summary; network/read/decode/warp timings separated |
| 1 | Presto, small fixed set of field-interior pixel time series; 12 months with truthful missing masks | CPU float32 first | Load/forward result, feature dimensions, finite outputs, no future frames |
| 2 | OlmoEarth v1.2 Nano, then Tiny: 128×128 native Sentinel-2; T=1 then 4 then 12 | CPU float32; explicit MPS float32 if available; optional MPS FP16 only after parity | Exact input shapes, model normalization, device/operator failures, CPU/MPS output differences, peak footprint |
| 3 | Prithvi tiny-TL, six-band HLS 224×224 and four eligible dates | CPU float32; explicit MPS float32 if supported | Coordinates/dates honored; encoder features, not MAE reconstructions treated as observed reflectance |
| 4 | OlmoEarth Small/Base or Prithvi 100M/300M only if a smaller feature model passes usefulness gate | Same one-chip protocol | Demonstrated held-out benefit over indices/Presto/smaller encoder justifies extra latency and memory |

For each eligible cell use one warm-up and five timed forward passes; cold load measured separately. Synchronize MPS before/after timing; log process RSS plus MPS allocated/driver memory without summing overlapping unified memory as independent pools. Capture OS memory pressure and swap change. Log inference/preprocessing/download separately. Initial proposed stop gates: 120 s per single forward or 8 GiB process footprint, sustained memory pressure, or accumulating swap; reduce spatial/time extent rather than completing a large matrix. These are conservative trial limits, not predicted performance. First test with the language model unloaded; only qualify co-residence afterward. A CPU fallback after an MPS operator failure is a separate result, not an MPS pass. No training or fine-tuning is in this matrix.

## Current-season, anomaly, and yield validity

Proposed dashboard: field outline, masked clear observation and acquisition date, index time series with gaps, clear-area fraction, crop/planting-date provenance, weather history, and historical comparator cohort. A model-derived pattern layer should carry its own legend and source/model revision. Missing imagery remains missing; an imputation or cloud reconstruction is displayed as a model product.

For a historical anomaly, compare like phenological stages and crop/rotation contexts, optionally using planting date and accumulated heat units with the base-temperature definition recorded. Do not compare a late-sown current crop to an unconditional calendar-date average. Maintain a fixed sensor/preprocessing baseline, report the number of eligible historical years/clear dates and spread, and retain long cloudy intervals. Predeclare a minimum cohort; for example, five eligible crop-years is a pilot target, not a universally sufficient climatology. A same-field baseline may have too few same-crop years; a regional analogue then needs an explicit domain label. Satellite constellation changes and historical QA/geolocation issues can mimic change.

For yield forecasting, first define crop, field-season target, harvested area, moisture/unit convention and forecast cut-off. Join quality-controlled yield outcomes, weather available by that cut-off, management/soil context and imagery available by that cut-off. Compare a crop-region-year/field-history baseline and index/weather model against frozen-embedding additions. Hold out farms/fields, regions and complete years; report per-crop MAE/RMSE/bias, interval coverage and skill relative to baseline. Neighboring chips of one field never become independent train/test examples. Full-year AlphaEarth embeddings or post-harvest imagery leak future information into an in-season forecast and must be excluded. OlmoEarth and AlphaEarth use CDL-derived supervision: crop-label benchmarks need a pretraining-overlap audit as well as downstream splits. [OlmoEarth modality statement](https://github.com/allenai/olmoearth_pretrain_minimal), [AlphaEarth training description](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/GOOGLE_SATELLITE_EMBEDDING_V1_ANNUAL).
