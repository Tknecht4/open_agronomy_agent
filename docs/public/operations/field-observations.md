# Useful field observations

When an answer cannot establish a diagnosis, rate or application window, use it
to identify the next observation that could change the decision. A label check
alone does not answer a scouting question. A safe observation plan also does not
authorize treatment.

For patchy crop symptoms, compare affected patch margins with nearby normal
areas and representative locations across the field. Record location, crop
stage, symptoms, incidence or severity and recent management. Dig intact plants
to compare roots and crowns and inspect soil moisture, drainage and compaction.
Keep contrasting samples separately identified, follow the diagnostic lab's
instructions and revisit marked locations. Symptoms can have biological,
weather, soil or application causes. See [Alberta field scouting](https://www.alberta.ca/index.php/field-scouting)
and [Manitoba sample submission instructions](https://www.gov.mb.ca/agriculture/crops/crop-diagnostic-services/pubs/sample-submission-instructions.pdf).

For a nutrient sampling plan, keep contrasting landscape and management zones
separate; an eroded knoll, wet depression and old manure strip need not be
represented by one whole-field average. Record the sampling depth and date and
keep handling and laboratory methods comparable. [Alberta's sampling guidance](https://www1.agric.gov.ab.ca/%24department/deptdocs.nsf/all/agdex1341)
and [British Columbia's sampling-zone guidance](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/agriculture-seafood/agricultural-land-and-environment/soil-nutrients/nutrient-management/what-to-apply/soil-nutrient-testing/soil-sampling-methods-tools)
support representative sampling. Test-specific depths, seasonal timing and
nutrient calibration still need the applicable local protocol.

Use the actual collection date when comparing sampling events across seasons.
Keep the laboratory report issuance date separately, linked to the sample and
original report. Issuance does not establish when laboratory processing or
analysis occurred; retain those dates separately when supplied.

Edge pest counts alone cannot establish whole-field density. Match a current
crop-and-pest threshold to the actual sampling units, representative interior
counts, growth stage, population trend and beneficial insects. Bloom and
pollinator restrictions still apply to an evening application. The
[Manitoba field-pea sampling guide](https://www.gov.mb.ca/agriculture/crops/insects/pubs/aphids-on-peas-factsheet-revised-june2023.pdf)
describes representative sampling; [Alberta's threshold guidance](https://www.alberta.ca/economic-thresholds-for-insects-attacking-special-crops)
also shows why crop value and control costs affect interpretation. Neither is
an exact product's current application label.

Matching threshold units means comparing the same counting basis; a per-plant
count is not a per-sweep count without a validated relationship. Treatment
thresholds alone do not establish whether a sample is representative or precise
enough. Sparse, variable or borderline observations can require more sampling,
and below-threshold populations may still need monitoring. See
[Minnesota scouting methods](https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/crop-production/soybean/scouting-for-soybean-aphid)
and [Iowa State scouting guidance](https://crops.extension.iastate.edu/encyclopedia/soybean-aphid).
Their crop-specific numerical thresholds are not transferred here.

A product rate describes the commercial formulation on its stated application
basis. A field nutrient application rate commonly describes nutrient mass per
area; other systems may use an explicitly stated concentration/volume basis.
Use the declared nutrient basis and product analysis for conversion. A pesticide
label specifies the applicable use directions and rate basis; an application
rate is distinct from a residue limit in food. See [EPA label guidance](https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-labels/introduction-pesticide-labels)
and [residue-tolerance definitions](https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-tolerances/setting-tolerances-pesticide-residues-foods).
A historical quantity does not authorize a current application, and a terminology
explanation does not select a product or field target.

Suspected late blight deserves prompt local diagnostic attention; collapse
after wet weather alone does not identify the cause. Inspect fresh foliage,
stems, roots and tubers. [Alberta's late-blight guidance](https://www.alberta.ca/late-blight-of-potatoes-and-tomatoes)
and [Cornell's lookalike guide](https://cce.cornell.edu/cornell-vegetable-program/late-blight-and-its-lookalikes-correctly-identify-late-blight-potatoes)
support identification, with product decisions subject to local authority.
For a healthy potato crop, monitor leaves and stems regularly and use local
alerts and crop-specific IPM guidance for prevention planning; visible collapse
is not a prerequisite. See [Minnesota late-blight monitoring](https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/specialty-crops/vegetable-farming/disease-management/late-blight)
for general scouting methods, with treatment authority still local.
For suspected clubroot, limit movement of contaminated soil while arranging
confirmation. One nonhost year does not establish eradication: resting spores
can persist for many years. Follow the [current applicable provincial plan](https://www.alberta.ca/alberta-clubroot-management-plan)
and any municipal requirements for the field. Check all susceptible brassica
crops, volunteers and cruciferous weeds when assessing a nonhost interval.

Treat regional crop-health and agroclimate layers as screening context. Confirm
the exact metric, date, spatial support and whether it is a modelled or observed
product. A regional anomaly over a reference period is not tomorrow's on-site
spray weather. Check [AAFC's product schedule](https://agriculture.canada.ca/en/agricultural-production/weather/agroclimate-maps/agroclimate)
and current local observations. For a satellite comparison, examine cloud and
shadow QA, valid pixels and coverage, comparable processing and spatial support,
and crop stage before attributing an NDVI difference to crop condition. See
[HLS quality documentation](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/1698/HLS_User_Guide_V2.pdf).
Identical sensor names are not required when a documented harmonization makes
the observations comparable. [NASA's HLS algorithms](https://hls.gsfc.nasa.gov/algorithms/)
explain cross-sensor corrections and shared spatial support; QA, crop stage and
remaining measurement uncertainty still need review.
An empty terrain clip also requires source coverage and acquisition-age checks;
see [Quebec terrain preparation](quebec-terrain.md).

One water sample is a dated observation. Interpret water EC and SAR together
with soil texture, infiltration, root-zone tests, crop stage and system
uniformity; repeat samples when source quality changes during the season.
SAR describes sodium relative to calcium and magnesium, while EC indicates
salinity. A water test alone does not diagnose soil sodicity or establish a
leaching prescription. See [Alberta agricultural water](https://www.alberta.ca/agricultural-water)
and its [irrigation procedures manual](https://www1.agriculture.alberta.ca/%24department/deptdocs.nsf/all/irr4437/%24FILE/procedures.pdf).

Check a guide's crop and production-system scope before using its tables.
Haskap (also called honeyberry or [camerise](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/ressources/bibliotheque/dictionnaires/VocabulairesPDF/terminologie_fruits.pdf)) is a perennial fruit crop: a vegetable spacing or fertilizer table does
not establish its layout or nutrient requirement. Mature size, equipment,
harvest access and compatible pollinizers matter. Ontario's
[haskap profile](https://omafra.gov.on.ca/CropOp/en/spec_fruit/berries/hask.html)
supports the crop distinction and explicitly limits transfer of regional
fertility guidance. It does not establish a British Columbia rate.
Pollination requirements are crop- and cultivar-specific: [haskap needs compatible
cross-pollination with overlapping bloom](https://gardening.usask.ca/gardening-advice/gardenline-nested-pages/food-plant-pages/fruit/haskap.php),
while [raspberries are generally self-fruitful](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/pollination-requirements-tree-and-small-fruits).
A pollinizer requirement for one perennial fruit must not become a universal
planting rule for other berries; insect pollination remains important.

These references support general observation methods. Their presence here
does not admit them into runtime retrieval or qualify a field recommendation.
